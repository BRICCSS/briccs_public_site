# Makoto Jones biosketch

> **Document purpose:** Source material for drafting a short public-facing biography of Dr. Makoto
> Jones for the BRICCS website. The text below was supplied for reference and has not yet been
> condensed or independently verified.

See also: [Makoto Jones: Contributions to Science](makoto-jones-contributions-to-science.md).

I am a health services researcher using "big data" to engage in scientific discovery and support
healthcare quality improvement, bringing perspectives from my other roles as infectious diseases
physician and informatician. By carefully analyzing the electronic health records (EHR) of tens of
millions of Veterans cared for by US Department of Veterans Affairs (VA), I address pressing
priorities in biosurveillance, antimicrobial stewardship, and infection control. I was involved in
extracting national microbiology and antimicrobial use data sets and making them available for
other scientists and VA quality programs. I am also the director of the Biosurveillance,
Antimicrobial Stewardship, and Infection Control Program within the Clinical Assessment,
Reporting, and Tracking (CART) Program. In this capacity we built and support, amongst other
things, the VA National Surveillance Tool for COVID-19 with related interagency reporting, as well
as healthcare-associated infection and antimicrobial use reporting to the US Centers for Disease
Control and Prevention (CDC). Along with these responsibilities, we have been actively researching
how these data can be used to improve surveillance, infection control, and antimicrobial use and
resistance.

To effectively address important questions, I have spent tremendous attention to understanding the
potential sources of bias from using secondary data and the use of methods to reduce them (e.g.,
natural language processing). I investigate questions about policy (e.g., screening for
methicillin-resistant *Staphylococcus aureus*) and program effects that incorporate new
technologies (e.g., implementation of antimicrobial time outs and carbapenem-resistant
*Enterobacteriaceae* tracking systems), pathogen transmission (including antibiotic effects), and
predictive analytic problems (to detect proven threat pathogens and outbreaks). I also conduct
cognitive analyses to understand how those receiving new information resources interpret them. I
participate in process-mining projects to generate human-intelligible clinical pathways with US
Department of Energy's Oak Ridge National Laboratories. I have led or lead projects using machine
learning to detect novel cases or diagnostic error. I led a project investigating the effectiveness
of provider-specific feedback for inpatient antimicrobial use in the current VA CARRIAGE QUERI and
will lead an evidence-based practice project investigating antimicrobial prescription for pneumonia
in the most recent QUERI renewal. This is related to CDC-supported work developing and validating
quality metrics.
