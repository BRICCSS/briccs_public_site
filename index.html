About BRICCS

Collaborative research for high-consequence public health challenges.

BRICCS brings together researchers from the VA Salt Lake City Health Care System, the University of Utah, and federal partners. The group develops and validates data-driven methods for high-consequence public health threats and extends the work of the VA's BASIC Program within the Clinical Assessment, Reporting, and Tracking (CART) Program.

Our approach connects subject-matter expertise, robust methods, and strong partnerships. We value scientific integrity, reproducibility, respectful collaboration, and clear communication.

Group leadership

Dr. Makoto Jones

BRICCS group leader

Dr. Jones leads BRICCS and directs the Biosurveillance, Antimicrobial Stewardship, and Infection Control Program within the VA's Clinical Assessment, Reporting, and Tracking (CART) Program.

As a health services researcher, infectious diseases physician, and informatician, he uses national VA electronic health record data to advance scientific discovery, healthcare quality improvement, biosurveillance, antimicrobial stewardship, and infection control.

Research priorities

Research priorities: Better ways to detect, measure, and respond.

Our research connects clinical data, analytic methods, and operational needs.

01

Clinical Analytics & Surveillance

We study changing epidemic patterns using high-frequency, low-lag data and time-varying methods that can adapt as conditions change.

02

Quality Measure Development

We use electronic health record data, machine learning, and anomaly detection to develop, validate, and monitor clinical quality measures.

03

Modeling & Prediction

We apply natural language processing and other models to large-scale VA clinical data to identify emerging disease signals earlier.

What guides us

Research objectives

  • Detect changes in epidemic patterns using high-frequency, low-lag data.
  • Develop and maintain clinical quality measures using machine learning.
  • Identify emerging disease signals in clinical text before they appear in diagnostic codes.
  • Estimate current infectious disease burden using near-real-time VA and community data.
  • Move research findings into practical use for clinical and public health partners.
